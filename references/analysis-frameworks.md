# Analysis Frameworks

Read this reference when the request asks for causal interpretation,
counterfactual probabilities, power analysis, professional-boundary review, or
a moral judgment about another person.

## 1. Five-Part Evidence Ledger

Use five explicit categories:

1. **Observed/documented:** a record or directly observed event.
2. **Recollected:** the narrator's memory; important but not independently
   verified.
3. **Inferred:** an explanation compatible with the facts.
4. **Later evidence:** information unavailable at the decision point.
5. **Counterfactual:** a proposed outcome on a path not taken.

Do not upgrade a recollection to a verified fact or an inference to intent.

## 2. Counterfactual Protocol

### Fix the information set

Write one sentence stating the decision date and what was knowable then. Keep
later outcomes in a separate column. This prevents hindsight from masquerading
as foresight.

### Define realistic branches

An alternative must have been available, acceptable enough to choose, and
supported by the resources the person actually had. "Find a perfect job" is
not a branch. "Accept an available transfer while preparing an exit" may be.

### Follow second-order effects

A choice changes more than one outcome. Track income, time, reputation,
learning, dependency, health, family pressure, and future options. A route that
avoids one loss may create another.

### Express likelihood honestly

Prefer relative labels such as "most plausible," "credible but secondary," and
"possible with weak support." If the user requests numbers, call them
subjective scenario estimates and show the assumptions. Do not imply a sample,
base rate, or statistical calibration that does not exist.

### Interpret later success correctly

Later success can show that a route was possible. It does not prove that:

- the original plan was sound
- prior suffering was necessary
- the same low-preparation attempt is repeatable
- every participant's conduct was justified
- failure would have meant permanent incapacity

## 3. Power and Family Interaction

Analyze four layers separately:

- **Geometry:** number, position, proximity, blocked movement, and who chose the
  setting.
- **Procedure:** who set the topic, controlled turns, interrupted, demanded an
  immediate answer, or allowed a pause.
- **Effect:** whether the arrangement reduced agency, produced pressure, or
  offered support.
- **Intent:** concern, coordination, control, hostility, or mixed motives.

Geometry and procedure can establish an unequal interaction. They do not by
themselves establish a conspiracy or malicious intent. Conversely, caring
intent does not make a coercive process equal.

## 4. Loyalty, Dependence, and Adaptation

Do not treat employment relationships as feudal allegiance. Distinguish:

- **gratitude:** recognition of past help
- **loyalty:** willingness to sustain a relationship at some cost
- **dependence:** limited alternatives and reliance on another person's power
- **adaptation:** changing alignment after a transfer, closure, or contract end
- **betrayal:** breach of a meaningful commitment involving deception or harm

Before endorsing a moral label, ask whether the person had agency, whether the
prior relationship was still operative, and whether they harmed or denounced
the former party to gain favor. Describe conduct before character.

## 5. Professional-Boundary Review

Separate the substantive concern from the method used to act on it. A person
may sincerely believe a decision is wrong while still crossing procedural,
clinical, confidentiality, or role boundaries. Likewise, a boundary error does
not prove that every later organizational response was fair.

Use this sequence:

1. identify the person's formal role and authority
2. identify the action and affected third parties
3. identify the proper escalation route
4. assess urgency and available alternatives
5. name the procedural error without inventing motive
6. assess the organization's response separately

## 6. Two-Direction Error Check

Before finalizing, test both failure modes:

- **Heroization:** suffering becomes destiny, persistence becomes proof, and
  success erases luck or mistakes.
- **Demonization:** every adverse event becomes coordinated persecution, every
  adaptation becomes betrayal, and uncertainty disappears.

A strong answer can be morally clear while remaining evidentially bounded.
