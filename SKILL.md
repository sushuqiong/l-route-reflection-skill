# L Route Reflection Skill

Use this skill for emotionally charged accounts of unstable institutions,
career rupture, mentor or family pressure, changing alliances, interrupted
training, recovery through portable capabilities, or later reinterpretation of
success and failure.

Do not use it to diagnose a person, verify an accusation from recollection
alone, or turn a private narrative into public material without a privacy pass.

## Non-Negotiable Boundaries

1. Separate observation, recollection, inference, later evidence, and
   counterfactual speculation.
2. Be candid without converting moral labels into findings.
3. Do not use a known outcome to inflate what was knowable or likely at an
   earlier decision point.
4. Treat effort, prior assets, institutional conditions, other people's
   decisions, timing, and luck as distinct contributors.
5. Default to de-identification. Never upload raw source material, private
   identifiers, patient information, local paths, or a custom denylist.
6. Preserve third-party privacy even when the narrator wants to be named.
7. If there is immediate danger, coercion, self-harm risk, or patient-safety
   risk, address safety before literary or strategic interpretation.

## Choose the Output Context First

- **Private analysis:** retain only the detail needed to reason accurately.
  State that the result is based on the narrator's account unless independently
  corroborated.
- **Limited sharing:** remove direct identifiers and reduce precise dates,
  places, titles, and rare event combinations.
- **Public output:** apply the release gate in
  [references/privacy-release.md](references/privacy-release.md). Aliases alone
  are not sufficient.

Do not silently broaden a private analysis into a memoir, post, repository
example, image prompt, or other public artifact.

## Core Workflow

### 1. Establish the question and time point

Identify what the user is asking: reconstruction, causal interpretation,
decision review, counterfactual, relationship judgment, or public adaptation.
For a past decision, state the historical information set: what was known then,
what was only learned later, and what remains uncertain.

### 2. Build a claim ledger

Classify material into:

- **Observed or documented:** directly experienced or supported by a record.
- **Recollected:** remembered by the narrator but not independently checked.
- **Inferred:** a plausible explanation of behavior or structure.
- **Later evidence:** facts learned after the decision point.
- **Counterfactual:** a claim about an unrealized path.

Do not let a vivid memory or repeated interpretation change categories without
new evidence.

### 3. Map the route without forcing a redemption arc

Useful stages can include entry, attachment, warning signal, rupture,
alternative route, temporary stabilization, exit, and reinterpretation. Use
only stages supported by the material. A later success does not prove that the
earlier suffering, institution, or strategy was necessary.

### 4. Analyze the mechanism

Ask which mechanism is actually supported:

- loss of organizational protection
- dependence on a leader or platform
- constrained adaptation to a transfer or contract ending
- accumulation of portable skills or credentials
- family or institutional pressure
- procedural or professional-boundary failure
- selection effects, timing, or luck

Distinguish a mechanism from a motive. A seating arrangement can create a
four-to-one pressure structure without proving a coordinated hostile plan.
Changing supervisors can be adaptive survival without proving betrayal.

### 5. Test competing explanations

Give at least one reasonable alternative explanation for consequential claims.
Evaluate both intent and effect: benign intent does not erase coercive impact,
and harmful impact does not by itself establish malicious intent.

### 6. Answer at the requested sharpness

Lead with the direct judgment. Then state its confidence and basis. Do not hide
an uncomfortable conclusion behind reassurance, but do not turn uncertainty
into theatrical certainty.

## Counterfactual Discipline

For questions such as "What if I had chosen differently?":

1. Fix the decision date and information available at that date.
2. Name only alternatives that were genuinely feasible.
3. State what is being held constant and what would probably change with the
   decision.
4. Compare short-, medium-, and long-term consequences.
5. Include opportunity costs and new risks, not only avoided harms.
6. Rank scenarios qualitatively unless a defensible numerical model exists.
7. If giving numbers, label them as subjective scenario estimates, explain the
   assumptions, and use ranges rather than false precision.
8. Use later events only as retrospective evidence about platform stability,
   not as information the person should have possessed earlier.

Read [references/analysis-frameworks.md](references/analysis-frameworks.md)
when the request involves probabilities, causal claims, family power,
professional boundaries, or moral labels.

## Privacy-Preserving Transformations

Prefer functional labels such as "a private institution," "the former unit
lead," "a receiving supervisor," or "a family member." Also generalize:

- exact dates to broader periods when precision is unnecessary
- rare specialties or units to functional categories
- exact amounts, scores, ranks, and project numbers to bounded descriptions
- searchable quotations to paraphrases
- distinctive sequences to the minimum events needed for the analysis

Never preserve identifying relational geometry merely by replacing names. A
unique institution, rare department, exact month, unusual incident, and later
outcome can re-identify people as a bundle.

Before public release, run the repository scanner and complete the manual rare-
combination review described in
[references/privacy-release.md](references/privacy-release.md).

## Output Shapes

Choose the smallest useful output:

- **Direct judgment:** conclusion, evidence boundary, competing explanation,
  practical implication.
- **Counterfactual review:** historical information set, feasible branches,
  near/mid/long-term consequences, confidence limits.
- **Timeline:** period, event, source status, contemporaneous interpretation,
  later interpretation, unresolved uncertainty.
- **Relationship analysis:** resources exchanged, dependency, alternatives,
  conduct, and whether a moral label is warranted.
- **Public-safe narrative:** de-identified route plus a separate privacy-risk
  note; do not expose the source chronology.

Use [templates.md](templates.md) for reusable structures. Use
[examples.md](examples.md) for abstract examples, never as evidence about a
real user.

## Quality Check

Before finishing, verify:

- Did the answer distinguish fact from motive and interpretation?
- Did it avoid hindsight bias and outcome worship?
- Did it preserve the user's own mistakes where relevant without using them to
  excuse other people's conduct?
- Did it distinguish adaptation, dependence, loyalty, gratitude, and betrayal?
- Did it identify what remains unknown?
- Is the public version non-identifying by combination, not merely by name?
- Does the answer serve the user's actual question rather than imposing a
  heroic, victim, or villain narrative?

The objective is not to prove that suffering was necessary. It is to identify
what happened, what can reasonably be inferred, what remained contingent, and
what the person carried forward despite the instability.
