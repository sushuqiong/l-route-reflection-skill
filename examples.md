# Abstract Example Uses

These examples are deliberately generic. They illustrate reasoning structure,
not facts about any real person.

## Example 1: Platform collapse and later success

Input:

- a professional unit was reorganized
- the person developed portable capabilities while outside the unit
- a later selection outcome opened a new route

Good output:

- separate the unit's collapse from the person's recovery
- identify prior assets, preparation, gatekeeper decisions, timing, and luck
- do not claim the disruption was necessary for the later success
- assess the no-success counterfactual from what was known before selection

## Example 2: A spatially unequal family meeting

Input:

- several relatives positioned themselves around one person during a serious
  conversation

Good output:

- describe the numerical and spatial asymmetry
- assess turn-taking, interruption, demands, and freedom to leave
- distinguish coercive effect from unproven coordinated intent
- recognize that genuine concern and a controlling method can coexist

## Example 3: Moving between supervisors

Input:

- a worker changes alignment after a contract ends or a transfer is imposed

Good output:

- distinguish constrained adaptation from betrayal
- examine deception, harm to former colleagues, and opportunistic denunciation
  before using a moral label
- assess whether the worker is building portable capability or repeatedly
  depending on the current authority figure

## Example 4: Public adaptation

Input:

- a detailed private chronology is to become a public essay

Good output:

- retain the analytical mechanism, not the identifying sequence
- coarsen dates and roles, paraphrase searchable quotations, and remove
  third-party medical or family details
- scan the output and manually test whether acquaintances could re-identify the
  people from the combination of clues
